Mute Chess: missed sacrifices versus similar ordinary offers
November 4, 2026 (Japan)

Conclusion

This investigation rejects several tempting shortcuts and identifies one modest
new feature hypothesis. It does NOT establish an improved classifier.

The additional question worth testing is: after the opponent accepts the offer,
how much worse is the player's second-best continuation than the best one?
That measures dependence on a precise compensating reply, rather than merely
detecting a material offer or recovery. Our current75features include a best/
second-best gap BEFORE the played move, but not this conditional response gap.

The matched evidence is suggestive, not decisive. The conditional gap was larger
for the Brilliant in16of20pairs with finite comparable evaluations, versus4in
the other direction. Four pairs involved forced mates and were not forced into
centipawn arithmetic. The preceding complete engine iteration gave the same
16/4split; those iterations are correlated, not independent replications.

There is substantial overlap: median gaps were365cp for Brilliants and288cp for
ordinary moves; a300cp cutoff would include11Brilliants and10ordinary controls.
Thus a simple large-gap rule is NOT supported. This is an input to test together
with existing context, not a new standalone award gate.

What was compared

The broad saved-data audit examined51missed Brilliants and149explicit hard-
ordinary moves from the fresh corpus. All passed current eligibility checks and
had ec_sacrifice=false. All ordinary labels were Great, Best, Excellent or Good;
missing labels and easy errors were excluded. Legacy mixed-label cases were
excluded from this diagnostic to keep provenance straightforward.

From that pool,24closest available cross-label pairs were selected using existing
features only. Each of48cases belonged to a different whole-game/position group.
Matching required the same capture/noncapture category, whether the moved piece
was offered, and offered minor/rook/queen categories. It also compared evaluation,
material, phase, history, and other existing measurements. Minor-piece matching
does not require bishop versus knight identity. Matching is approximate, not a
claim that positions are identical. The greedy nearest-pair sample favors cases
with close available controls and is not representative of play or all misses.

Existing saved lines omitted every accepting reply for11of24Brilliants and7of24
ordinary cases. A bounded probe analyzed all62qualifying accepting captures from
the48positions, adding27branches absent from the saved top-two continuations.
This used the same pinned Stockfish18,300,000nodes per requested position,
one thread,64MiB hash, up to2complete same-depth roots, and full PGN history.
Hash was cleared before each conditional search. All searches finished in17.3s.
No new labels were collected and no classifier was fit or deployed.

For case-level comparisons, the opponent's best accepting capture was used:
the capture with the lowest resulting evaluation for the player making the
candidate move. Branch choice did not depend on the Brilliant/ordinary label.
All branches, including alternatives, remain in PROBES.json.

What did not distinguish the labels

In the matched24vs24sample:
                                       Brilliant       Ordinary
Recover material on first reply        18/24           19/24
Acceptance permits forced mate          3/24            3/24
Material deficit>100 after6plies,
  excluding forced-mate cases            2/24            2/24
Median material debt at6plies           -90             -90

Positive debt means material invested relative to just before the played move;
negative debt means net material gained. The6/12ply horizons include acceptance.
First-reply recovery can be followed by further losses; it is not an end-state
claim. Terminal mate outcomes remain separately identified. One ordinary12ply
endpoint was unknown because its saved conditional PV ended early.

The broader saved-PV audit independently reinforces the recovery counterexample:
acceptance lines existed for33/51missed positives and78/149ordinary controls;
24of33positives and54of78ordinary moves recovered material within6plies on at
least one saved acceptance line. No saved acceptance means unknown consequences,
not no compensation.

Concrete cases

Pair2: two Nxe5 moves expose a knight, and acceptance allows ...Bxd1, winning the
opponent's queen. The Brilliant case (173661079138:26) was played by Hikaru3373;
the Best case (147329069640:16) by nelsi2598. The acceptance lines start:
  Brilliant: Nxe5 Bxd1 Nxd1 Kf8
  Best:      Nxe5 Bxd1 Rxd1 Qe7
These are different positions. The shared motif and immediate recovery do not
settle the label; the ordinary case even has the larger conditional gap.

Pair10: Nd3 (170661862908:40), labeled Brilliant, and Ne3 (184277876260:54),
labeled Great, both allow QxN Qxg2#. Their overall evaluations and player
ratings differ. Forced mate if the offer is accepted is insufficient by itself.

Pair15: Rxb3+ (172914653473:86), Brilliant, and Rxe5 (184330910250:75), Great,
both have110of existing3ply physical-debt evidence. The new conditional lines:
  Brilliant: Kxb3 Nxe3 Bb7 a5 Ba6 Nf5
  Great:     dxe5 Rxd5 c4 Rc5 b3 cxb3
The first retains110at6plies; the second retains20. Longer trajectories help
describe the difference, but lasting debt does not separate the full sample.
The new Great continuation differs from its old saved best line, illustrating
why a horizon-based recoup feature can change with the chosen engine line.

Pair23: Nxe5 (173851185684:34), Brilliant, leaves a rook available to Nxb6.
The original top-two lines declined the offer. The new acceptance line starts
Nxb6 axb6 c5 bxc5 and remains favorable to the mover. Its ordinary matched
hxg5 (167275575688:60) also offers a rook and supports a favorable continuation.
The ordinary offer was already present in prior history; that warning is already
in the existing features. Neither accepting-capture desirability nor history
should be turned into an unconditional shortcut.

Interpretation and next bounded test

Some controls contain real tactical sacrifices too. Detecting a sacrifice is
therefore not enough to reproduce the target's award boundary. The findings do
not justify reclassifying controls as mislabeled or discarding inconvenient
positive labels.

The next feature experiment should add conditional best/second-response evidence
to the same original inputs and compare it under the same grouped evaluation
and wrong-award constraints. Report the ec_sacrifice=false subgroup separately.
The newly examined48cases are development examples and cannot provide fresh
validation for a rule inspired by them. Compute any added features symmetrically
for positives and negatives using a predeclared search budget and missing-value
policy. Do not select cases for extraction using target labels.

If that adds no useful held-out recall, the evidence would favor examining
target-label conditions and engine/rating context before more acceptance-feature
engineering. Several same-motif pairs remain unexplained. The saved labels were
not re-reviewed in Chess.com during this task, and actual review settings were
not experimentally controlled. Rating also remains an unmatched contextual
variable: positives had lower ratings in13pairs and higher in11, so a simple
lower-rating explanation does not account for this panel.

Important limits

This is an exploratory matched case-control investigation. Its percentages are
not estimates of recall, precision, or deployment error. Multiple descriptive
features were examined after selection; the conditional-gap finding needs a
separate evaluation. Larger conditional gaps might reflect tactical necessity
without matching Chess.com's specific label rule. Acceptance-vs-original-score
differences compare different engine searches and must not be read as exact.
Engine PVs show analyzed continuations, not a proof covering every defense.
