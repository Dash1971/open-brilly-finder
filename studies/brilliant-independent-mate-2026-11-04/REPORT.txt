Independent acceptance-mate feature experiment
November 4, 2026

RESULT

Removing the En Croissant prerequisite from the mating feature did not find
more Brilliants without increasing wrong awards. Do not promote this change
on the evidence from this experiment. No deployed model or classifier changed.

Same grouped held-out evaluation, all917 saved Brilliants and2,807 negatives:

Method                              Correct   Recall   Wrong   Wrong/awards
Custom forest, original              452      49.29%     6       1.31%
Custom forest, corrected feature     449      48.96%     6       1.32%
Standard RF, original                473      51.58%     7       1.46%
Standard RF, corrected feature       462      50.38%     7       1.49%

The primary custom comparison gains2 correct awards and loses5, for3 fewer.
Its six wrong awards are exactly unchanged. The secondary standard forest
gains1 correct and loses12; it replaces one wrong award with a different one.
Thus unchanged total wrong awards does not mean every wrong decision is the
same for the standard forest.

The predeclared custom fixed5/6 control has the same totals before and after:
436 correct and6 wrong. It gains4 correct awards and loses4. This control also
shows no net benefit; we did not select a favorable cutoff after seeing results.

WHAT CHANGED

Old feature:
    mating_acceptance = !ec_sacrifice && hasMatingAcceptance(before, after)
Corrected feature:
    mating_acceptance = hasMatingAcceptance(before, after)

The existing helper is unchanged: it seeks a literal legal immediate checkmate
after accepting a qualifying nonpawn offer, with the same512-position proof
budget and false-on-exhaustion behavior. This is not an expanded search for
longer forced mates, a new unknown-status feature, or an automatic award rule.

We invoked it for all3,724 saved positions without label, score, or eligibility
selection. Original masked values reproduced exactly for every case. The
correction changes67 cases from false to true (134 window rows); none change
from true to false. Among64 fully eligible changed cases:
    22 were missed Brilliants under the original custom selected cutoffs;
    31 were already awarded Brilliants;
    11 were ordinary moves.
The remaining three changed cases were ineligible: one positive and two negatives.
All64 eligible changes also have independently replayed, legally valid immediate
mate witnesses in the saved acceptance evidence. No engine searches were needed.

Only the existing mating_acceptance column was replaced. The other74 feature
values, all75 missing indicators, column order, original eligibility/quality
checks, row weights, two-window confirmation, confidence and physical-unknown
guards stayed identical. The earlier evaluation-consistency gate proposal was
not incorporated. This isolates the mating-feature correction.

DID IT HELP THE CASES THAT MOTIVATED IT?

Of the22 original misses whose feature was corrected, the custom forest now
awards one. Among the31 previously found Brilliants with corrected features,
it loses one. None of the11 changed ordinary cases receives an award.

Outside the feature-flipped cases, refitting and inner threshold selection
gain one correct award and lose four. These indirect effects explain the
remaining regression. A changed training feature can alter tree splits and
cutoffs even for test examples whose own feature did not change.

Across all429 original eligible custom misses, the corrected custom finds two,
but it loses five previously correct awards elsewhere. This subgroup is
descriptive, selected from the same original held-out labels and decisions;
it is not an independent validation set.

The original difficult EC-false subgroup remains7/191 found with the custom
forest. Correcting the mask directly affects EC-true cases; it does not supply
a different representation of the EC-false sacrifice mechanisms. The eligible
EC-true custom results decline from445 correct to442, with six wrong unchanged.

EVALUATION DESIGN

Same3,724 cases,917 positives,972 whole-game/canonical-position group components,
five outer folds and three inner folds as the prior learner comparisons. Aliases,
repeated positions, games and both windows remain grouped.

For this input experiment, both original and corrected versions were refit
under identical settings and seeds. Custom:64 trees, depth6, minimum8 distinct
moves per leaf,12 candidate columns, group bootstrap, class-cost5 splits and
raw bootstrap-window leaf proportions. Standard RF:128 trees, depth6,
minimum16 rows per leaf,12 candidate columns, one thread, original weights.
The total input width remains150. No learner parameters or feature definition
were adjusted after outcomes.

Each selected cutoff uses only the outer training partition's inner held-out
predictions. The unchanged selection rule maximizes correct awards subject to
BOTH no higher wrong count AND no worse wrong-award fraction than the original
custom fixed5/6 reference on those inner predictions. That training constraint
does not guarantee it will hold on outer held-out games. All outer outcomes,
including regressions, are reported.

Primary: corrected custom versus original retuned custom.
Secondary: corrected standard RF versus original standard RF.
Secondary control: corrected versus original custom at fixed5/6.
No learner-selection procedure, final full fit, already-seen benchmark replay,
fresh label collection, deployment, or outcome-driven rerun was performed.

80 fits completed in51.2 seconds, peak resident memory approximately300 MB.
Both original learner score vectors reproduced exactly (maximum difference0),
and every original baseline award reproduced on all3,724 rows.

UNCERTAINTY AND INTERPRETATION

The paired group-bootstrap95% interval for the primary net correct count is
[-8,+2], approximately[-0.88,+0.22] recall percentage points. Wrong-count
difference is[0,0] because every original negative decision is unchanged here;
this does not guarantee unchanged errors on new data. The standard RF net
correct interval is[-19,-5], and its wrong-count interval is[-3,+3]. These
intervals condition on fitted predictions and omit refitting and accumulated
research-selection uncertainty.

The corrected feature states the chess fact more faithfully, but that fact
occurs in both classes and is not by itself a useful award distinction in this
test. We have no evidence here for more recall from unmasking it alone. The
experiment does not prove that all mating information is useless, that richer
conditional structure cannot help, or that the entire matching ceiling has
been reached. It does narrow the proposed simple encoding fix: under the
tested learners and evaluation, it fails the desired improvement criterion.

This corpus is enriched and repeatedly studied. These are grouped development
results, not live-site performance estimates or fresh prospective confirmation.
The experiment refits research learners; it is not a reevaluation of the deployed
848-example model on its training data. Ratings were not used.
