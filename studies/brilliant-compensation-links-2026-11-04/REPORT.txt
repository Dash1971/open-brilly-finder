Representing the connection between a sacrifice and its compensation
November 4, 2026

FINDING

There is useful chess information missing from the current scalar features:
which particular piece accepts an offer, where it moves, and how that change
participates in the compensating continuation. We can record those connections
from saved legal lines without another engine search.

Some narrow relations look worth investigating as learned inputs. The most
interesting here is acceptance relocating a piece into the very next fork-shaped
attack. Generic path clearance and direct recapture overlap heavily between
Brilliants and ordinary moves. No classifier was fitted and no accuracy gain is
established. These descriptions do not yet recover Chess.com's award boundary.

THE REPRESENTATION

Keep a small record per acceptance branch, rather than reducing different offers
and recoveries immediately to a single maximum gain or minimum material debt:

  Candidate move and offered piece
       -> accepting piece, origin, destination
       -> exact occupancy/attack changes caused by that capture
       -> legal saved response(s), checks, captures, pins and fork targets
       -> witnessed material/mating outcome and remaining uncertainty.

Piece identity persists when the acceptor moves again or is later captured.
The record distinguishes capturing that acceptor from recovering material by
taking some OTHER piece. It also distinguishes a recapture that creates a new
attack from a simple recapture ending an exchange.

Five concepts must remain separate:
  1. Acceptance vacates a blocker on the saved reply's path.
  2. Acceptance replaces our offered piece with an enemy piece on that square,
     allowing a saved recapture or relocating a target into an attack.
  3. The response creates a new attack, pin or fork involving that target.
  4. The candidate move already created the threat; acceptance merely allows
     the saved continuation. The capture did not create the threat's geometry.
  5. No connection is witnessed within the saved line. That is unresolved,
     not proof that the offer is irrelevant.

We compare geometry on actual legal board states. We do not flip the side to
move to invent an illegal counterfactual. A geometric attack is not necessarily
a legal winning capture; a saved principal variation is not proof that every
defense loses. Root-created threats are manually verified in the panel, while
the automatic prototype concentrates on acceptance and response relations.

CONCRETE EXAMPLES

1. Accepting piece becomes a fork target: missed Brilliant ...Nxf4
   Case183799110991:36, panel7.
   ...Nxf4 Rxf4 ...e5.
   The knight offer brings White's rook from f1 to f4. Black's pawn move e6-e5
   attacks that exact accepting rook on f4 and the bishop on d4. The saved line
   continues Rff1 exd4 Nxd4. This is a precise connection from the offer to
   compensation; "knight offered" plus "material recovered" loses that link.

   Its ordinary control167744579866:12 has ...Nxe4 Nxe4 ...Qe7. The accepting
   knight moves c3-e4; Qd8-e7 pins it to Ke1, then ...d5 attacks it. This is
   real compensation too, but a different relationship. Earlier prose called
   ...d5 a fork of two knights; board replay disproves that description.
   See CORRECTIONS.txt. The distinction does not by itself explain the label.

2. Recapture becomes the attack: missed Brilliant ...Ba6
   Case174936338093:56, panel11.
   The move leaves an already offered rook on d4. Nxd4 replaces that rook with
   the accepting knight from e2. Black's e5xd4 then captures the acceptor and
   forks White's rook on c3 and queen on e3. The chain is:
       rook offer -> knight on d4 -> pawn captures on d4 -> attacks Rc3/Qe3.
   The acceptor is already gone when the fork exists; a feature asking only
   whether the acceptor is attacked would miss this second mechanism.

   Ordinary Bd3 (172914653473:53) also leaves Rd4 offered, but ...Bxd4 Nxd4
   is a direct recapture with no corresponding fork from Nd4. Both regain
   material, and the current numerical summaries obscure the difference.

3. Path clearance also belongs to ordinary tactics
   Ordinary Bxd5 (166753669376:43), panel3, allows ...Bc4xd5. The bishop
   vacates c4, the sole blocker between Rc1 and Qc8. Rxc8 then captures the
   queen. This is an especially clear occupancy certificate, yet the label
   is ordinary. Detecting a causal-looking connection is not sufficient.

   The paired Brilliant Bxg6 (166180997254:61) allows ...f7xg6 Qxg6+.
   Acceptance replaces the friendly bishop on g6 with an enemy pawn and
   clears f7; later ...Qf7 is met by Rxf7 on the opened f-file. But Qxg6+
   was already legal in the ORIGINAL position before Bxg6. Comparing only
   after-offer and after-acceptance positions would miss this earlier access.

4. Mate does not imply acceptance created the attack
   Ordinary ...Ng4 (180017464974:20), panel10, permits gxh3 Qh2#.
   The queen's e5-f4-g3-h2 ray is already clear after ...Ng4, and Ng4 already
   supports h2. Taking Bh3 does not open that ray or create the knight support.
   It permits a real mate using already-present geometry.

   Paired Brilliant ...Nf6 (186052437796:18) uses fxe4 Bc5, pinning Qd4 to
   Kf2; Qxc5 Nxe4+ then captures the acceptor and forks king/queen, followed
   by Nxc5. This more extended connection is represented, but the ordinary
   move's simpler mate remains a valid contradictory example.

WHAT WAS MEASURED

Using the existing saved evidence, we extracted relations for all1,302 cases
that pass the original eligibility/quality/confidence/physical checks. This
includes429 missed Brilliants,452 detected Brilliants and421 ordinary/negative
cases. Extraction does not use labels or classifier scores.

The evidence covers1,461 qualifying acceptance branches, both saved analysis
windows and up to two response PVs per branch. All5,836 full PVs were legally
replayed, totaling85,945 moves. Relations are recorded through the first six
response plies after acceptance, while full-PV legality is checked. Castling,
en passant, promotion and later movement of the acceptor preserve identity.

For case-level comparisons we select the opponent's best saved acceptance,
using the mover's least favorable evaluated outcome, and the best saved reply
to that acceptance. Mate outcomes retain mate ordering rather than being turned
into arbitrary centipawns. All other branches remain in RELATIONS.json. This
avoids choosing the most spectacular line or weighting a case more heavily
just because it has more accepting captures.

We reuse the prior429 ordinary matches, including their157 flagged weak
fallbacks. The primary comparison is the272 pairs with matching basic semantic
anchors (EC status, capture/check status, moved/offered-piece categories).
Those272 pairs use107 distinct ordinary moves; controls are reused, so pair
occurrences are correlated. Full boards are not identical.

Selected current-window observations in those272 comparisons:

The21 immediate-acceptor-fork misses are21 moves in20 groups. The seven

but19/21 missed cases and3/4 unique ordinary cases use a checking reply.
The difference may partly reflect checking tempo rather than target identity
alone. That possible confound has not been isolated by a learner experiment.

The ordinary fork cases are retained explicitly:
  168772277524:48,171306023703:39,173391055464:72,184277876260:142.
They include queen, rook and pawn attacks involving the acceptor; they are not
invalid examples or silently discarded exceptions.

Across the broader, unmatched case pools, the same immediate-acceptor-fork
relation is observed in38/429 misses and6/226 explicit hard ordinary controls.
These pools differ in composition; they must not be read as precision/recall.
Only a minority of misses have this particular relation. Path clearance and
direct recapture remain common on both sides, reinforcing why generic scalar
extensions have struggled.

WHAT THIS ADDS BEYOND THE CURRENT FEATURES

The existing75 measurements record offered roles, counts, aggregate exchange
gains/debts, quality and some historical attribution. They do not preserve the
acceptor's identity and location through the reply, the exact target pair of a
fork, which blocker was removed, or the sequence connecting one gain to another.
Two branches can therefore have similar totals but very different mechanisms.

The prototype demonstrates that these descriptions can be recovered precisely
and reproducibly from saved lines. It does NOT demonstrate predictive value
beyond the current inputs. A relational learned-input experiment would need to
compare them with the existing features under grouped evaluation, include the
ordinary counterexamples, and then face fresh validation. We have not fitted
such a model or selected an award cutoff here.

Of the relations examined, participation of the accepting piece in the immediate
compensating attack is the most concrete candidate for that future test. It
should be considered with reply check status, target roles and the relation's
known/unknown status. The broader branch record is useful for retaining evidence;
we should not assume every recorded relation must become a model input.

LIMITS AND VERIFICATION

Geometric forks include attacks on the king and attacks by pinned pieces;
neither target is guaranteed to be won. Pins are absolute geometric lines
against the king, not a full evaluation of relative pins. Absence means no
witness in the saved prefix, not proof of no compensation. In the current
response set,19 lines end nonterminal before six plies and262 terminate earlier;
their coverage is recorded. Immediate-reply metrics have an actual first reply
for every selected case, so the main21/7 observation is not a six-ply truncation
artifact.

FEN-only input lacks earlier repetition history. Local legality, checkmate,
stalemate and insufficient material can be checked; a complete historical draw
claim cannot. Saved engine branches do not cover all legal replies, all possible
acceptances under other definitions, or all realistic alternatives to the root.
Root-created versus already-existing threats were inspected manually in selected
examples and are not exhaustively extracted by this prototype.

Definitions/scripts/input hashes were frozen before extraction/comparison.
An independent review checked all2,604 case/window acceptance selections and
response score ordering, the main pair counts and geometric certificates in
five panel pairs. No branch-ranking or metric errors were found. It did correct
the earlier prose error about the ordinary pawn fork, preserved separately.

No new engine searches, model fits, rules, cutoffs, labels, ratings, app changes
or deployment. This is diagnostic work on enriched, repeatedly studied data,
not independent validation or a claim about matching Chess.com's private logic.
