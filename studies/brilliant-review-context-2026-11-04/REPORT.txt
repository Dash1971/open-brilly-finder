Matched tactics: evaluation and review-context investigation, 2026-11-04

Conclusion
Evaluation context explains some apparent similarities, but neither deeper local
search nor the tested Chess.com review settings removes the central Brilliant /
ordinary distinction. The remaining question is the definition and attribution
of a qualifying sacrifice. This is a diagnostic study, not an accuracy test.
Ratings were excluded from analysis and proposed classification rules. No Mute
code, model, training data, deployment, or existing experiment was changed.

Saved evidence
The fixed sample is 24 matched pairs / 48 distinct game-position groups. At the
exact target plies, 43 cases have visible-review evidence and 32 have explicit
review-export NAG evidence; 27 have both and all27 agree. Saved Chess.com settings
are absent. Local before/after depths are Stockfish evidence, not review settings.
Only12 cases retain a displayed Chess.com evaluation. With white-perspective
scores normalized to the original mover, their mean absolute difference from the
saved local after_cp is39.75cp; signed mean difference is-22.75cp. This small subset
cannot establish whole-corpus engine agreement. See PROVENANCE.json.

Tenfold local search
Frozen 96 searches: before and after each of48 targets, full PGN history, pinned
Stockfish18,3Mnodes versus saved300k, Threads1,Hash64,MultiPV2, clear hash each
search, complete same-depth root windows. Completed in295.9s. No retry or fitting.
Six before-position and five after-position scores shifted more than50cp among
raw-cp-comparable positions (46 before /47 after); mate transitions are separate.
Nine unique cases shifted >50cp at either stage; four >100cp. Only2 ordinary moves
crossed5pp loss in the project's probability mapping; no Brilliant did. This is
not Chess.com's expected-points model. Cross-position search scores can disagree
with same-search root scores; those are preserved separately.

Eight pairs still meet the frozen descriptive closeness criterion: after-score
shifts <=50cp for both, losses <=5pp, and pair score gap <=100cp. Examples:
- Pair8: BrilliantNxe4 -11cp, ordinaryNxg3 -30cp; both best roots.
- Pair20: BrilliantRxe5 +672cp, ordinaryRxf4+ +715cp; both best roots.
  (This pair exceeds the separate <=50cp stability condition, but remains close.)
- Pair23: BrilliantNxe5 +309cp, ordinaryhxg5 +304cp; both best roots.
- Pair15: BrilliantRxb3+ +375cp, ordinaryRxe5 +289cp; both best roots.
  Best other roots score+80cp and-20cp respectively: both sacrifices matter.
Other BEFORE roots above+200cp occur for11 Brilliant and7 ordinary cases; these
roots were not freshly checked for material safety. A generic 'another winning
move exists' veto is not justified by this study.

The winning queen-move pair11 has mate/horizon instability: Qh3 becomes mate12
after the move, while Qh6 has a before-position mate14 alternative but a finite
after-position score. Neither should be reduced to a precise cp cutoff.
Raw evidence, protocol, counts and independent score-sign/arithmetic verification

Implication and smallest next experiment
Keep the existing learner/evaluation foundation. Standardize and record review
engine/strength for future labels; keep historical setting provenance unknown.
Do not expect blanket depth increases to resolve the hard sacrifice labels.

One concrete clue survives in pair15's already-saved acceptance branches. With
project material values, Rxb3+ Kxb3 Nxe3 leaves110cp of material debt at6plies and
200cp at12. Rxe5 dxe5 Rxd5 followed by pawn exchanges leaves20cp at6 and12plies.
The earlier3-ply physical feature assigns110cp to both. This illustrates how
settled material cost can differ when the initial tactic and evaluation match.
It is a hypothesis, not an inferred Chess.com rule: many Brilliant moves recover
material immediately, and earlier broad trajectory counts overlapped heavily.

A bounded next ablation could add a small fixed bundle from the acceptance lines
already saved for the conditional-gap experiment: material debt after exchanges,
recoup timing, and terminal/mate status, together with existing features. Reuse
the exact grouped evaluation and threshold-selection constraint on wrong awards;
report the hard missed-sacrifice subgroup separately. Do not tune cutoffs to these
48 explored cases. This would test whether the clue generalizes at low extraction
cost, not assume that it does. No such learner experiment was run in this study.

Public reference context
Chess.com says Brilliant requires a good sacrifice, best/nearly-best play, an
acceptable resulting position, and not already being completely winning without
finding the move. Exact proprietary thresholds are not public in these sources:
https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8572705-how-are-moves-classified-what-is-a-blunder-or-brilliant-etc
Chess.com also says review strength and quick-vs-server review can change labels:
https://support.chess.com/en/articles/11845102-why-did-my-move-classification-change-in-game-review
Neither source proves a cause for a particular matched case.
