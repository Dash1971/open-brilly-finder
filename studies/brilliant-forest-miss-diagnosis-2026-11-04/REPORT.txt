Why the forest scores 429 eligible Brilliants too low
November 4, 2026

MAIN FINDING

There are two substantial failure patterns, not one missing sacrifice test.
The forest strongly favors the En Croissant material-simulation definition of
sacrifice. Among moves that satisfy it, the forest is much more successful on
captures that offer the moved piece than on quiet moves, stationary-piece
offers, and converted-material/endgame positions. The missed Brilliants often
look like ordinary moves under these summaries.

We found a concrete encoding gap and several kinds of information discarded
from the saved tactical lines. We have NOT established that a new descriptor
will distinguish Chess.com's labels reliably. Matched ordinary moves retain
real forks, promotions, king attacks and mating punishments. These contradictory
examples are preserved rather than explained away.

No award rule, cutoff, model choice, feature input, or deployed classifier was
changed. No new engine searches, labels, or learner experiment were performed.
Original fold forests were reconstructed solely to inspect their fixed paths.

WHAT WAS AUDITED

The target is exactly the429 eligible, below-cutoff positives from the previous
custom_retuned grouped evaluation, not the445 misses under the fixed5/6 cutoff.
All3,724 original held-out scores were reproduced EXACTLY, maximum error0, using
the original five outer training splits, seeds, settings and input values.
Both window scores and every traversed decision path are archived.

The score is the average training-label fraction in reached tree leaves, with
the lower of two engine-window scores used for awards. It is not a calibrated
probability that Chess.com will award Brilliant. Original split construction
penalizes negatives5x, but leaf scores use raw bootstrap-window proportions.
Path contributions telescope from each root's raw fraction to its reached leaf;
they describe associations in those fixed paths, not causal effects of changing
one feature. Correlated features can divide the same underlying association.

1. TWO DIFFERENT MISS POPULATIONS

En Croissant sacrifice feature  Eligible Brilliants  Found   Missed
True                                      690       445      245
False                                     191         7      184

That is64.5% found in the first subgroup versus3.7% in the second. The feature
is learned input, not a hard veto, but it behaves as strong evidence. Its
correlated ec_material_before/after/margin measurements provide additional
paths for the same broad material proxy to influence the score.

For the184 EC-false misses, the largest negative mean path associations are
ec_material_margin, ec_sacrifice and ec_material_after. These are not three
independent chess discoveries. They describe different measurements of a
similar sacrifice calculation. Simply turning one flag on would create a
different input distribution and is not a tested or recommended repair.

For the245 EC-true misses, that sacrifice evidence is already positive. Their
negative path associations instead commonly involve offered_piece_moved,
improvement over the preceding own move, immediate capture/material credit,
and converted_material_or_endgame. This group already has a recognized offer;
the forest lacks sufficiently reliable separation from ordinary tactics.

Within EC-true cases, current-window descriptive proportions are:

Property                         Found445    Missed245   Hard ordinary77
Offered piece itself moved       415(93.3%)  126(51.4%)    40(51.9%)
Move is a capture                371(83.4%)  112(45.7%)    35(45.5%)
Converted-material/endgame flag    13(2.9%)    76(31.0%)    44(57.1%)

The first two properties are almost equally common in misses and ordinary
controls. Their rarity in the detected-Brilliant group reveals a learned
preference for a narrow kind of sacrifice, but does not make a quiet-move or
stationary-offer exception safe. These are associations and overlapping groups.

2. THIS IS MOSTLY NOT WINDOW INSTABILITY OR A TINY CUTOFF SHORTFALL

418/429 misses score below their saved cutoff in BOTH windows. Only11 have one
window above it. The median missed score is0.6785, compared with0.9181 for
detected Brilliants and0.4455 for the226 explicit hard ordinary controls.
Median missed score is0.6039 with EC false and0.7329 with EC true.

38 misses lie within0.025 of their cutoff;119 are0.025–0.10 below;272 are more
than0.10 below. These descriptive bins were fixed before matching. There is no
cutoff sweep or recommendation to lower a threshold.

The reached leaves are mixed: their mean ordinary fraction is34.2% for misses,
9.0% for detected Brilliants and55.2% for the explicit ordinary control pool.
This partly restates how a forest score is calculated. It explains the mechanism
of low scores, not why the underlying labels differ. Misses reach leaves with
about53 distinct training groups on average, so there is no evidence that they
all rest on only one or two training games. Whether the mixture reflects missing
inputs, inadequate decision structure, sampling, or a combination remains open.

3. COMPARISON WITH ORDINARY MOVES

Each of429 misses was matched to a saved eligible hard ordinary move in the SAME
outer fold and a DIFFERENT game/position group. The primary pool contains226
explicit hard-ordinary negatives. All193 eligible legacy negatives are retained
in a separate comparison; two eligible easy-error negatives are not used as
ordinary tactical controls. This is not a performance evaluation or exclusion
from the earlier accuracy calculation.

Matching favors the same EC flag, capture/check status, whether the offered
piece moved, and offered minor/rook/queen categories. It then compares all75
existing feature values over both windows using fixed normalized distances,
equally weighted across six feature families. Neither forest score nor truth
is used in that distance. Scores/labels do define the diagnostic pools.

272 misses have controls satisfying those exact semantic anchors. For157,
no such control exists in the same-fold pool; their closest fallback is flagged
and is NOT claimed to be a close tactical analogue. All429 remain included.
The primary matching uses134 distinct controls, up to16 reuses; comparisons
are correlated and cannot support independent-pair significance claims.

The missed Brilliant scores higher than its primary ordinary control in325/429
comparisons, including194/272 exact-anchor comparisons. Thus the forest often
already sees a difference, but not enough to meet the conservative cutoff.
The median pair shares23.4% of the128 corresponding tree/window leaf paths
(27.3% among exact-anchor pairs). No pair shares every leaf. These are not
identical model representations or proof of irreducible label ambiguity.

We inspected a prespecified12-pair panel, two per EC flag x score-shortfall
stratum, with24 distinct groups. The two EC-false near-cutoff pairs have no exact
anchor controls and are poor comparisons; their limitations remain explicit.
For all24 cases,208 saved after-position and acceptance PVs were legally
replayed. No future played continuation was used as a predictor.

Selected concrete examples from the panel:

* Brilliant ...Nxf4 and ordinary ...Nxe4 both recover a knight through pawn
  forks. Nxf4 Rxf4 e5 forks a rook and bishop immediately. Nxe4 Nxe4 Qe7
  prepares ...d5, forking two knights after an e-file pin. Their forest scores
  are.813 and.642. Target pieces, pin and move order are real distinctions,
  but a generic fork flag would describe both. The label difference remains
  unexplained by this example alone.

* Brilliant ...Ba6 leaves a rook offered: Nxd4 exd4 forks queen and rook.
  Ordinary Bd3 also leaves a rook offered but uses ...Bxd4 Nxd4, a direct
  exchange recovery. The missed Brilliant scores lower (.507 versus.610).
  The causal link from vacating/capturing one piece to attacking two other
  pieces is absent from the scalar offer/material description.

* Brilliant ...Nf6 uses fxe4 Bc5, pinning the queen against the king, followed
  by Qxc5 Nxe4+ and Nxc5 to recover the queen. The ordinary ...Ng4 instead
  punishes gxh3 with Qh2#. The ordinary has the immediate mate. This is a
  direct counterexample to treating the more spectacular acceptance outcome
  as sufficient evidence for Brilliant.

* Brilliant Bg2 and ordinary Bh6 score almost identically (.762 versus.759).
  The ordinary acceptance continuation includes a promotion with check and
  eventual queen recovery. Ordinary Qg5 in another pair also has a forcing
  king attack. These are substantive sacrifices/tactics, not empty threats.

All12 comparisons, including endgame composition differences and unresolved
reverse examples, are in PANEL-AUDIT.txt. They expose missing descriptions,
not a discovered Chess.com decision rule.

4. A CONCRETE ENCODING GAP

The existing feature named mating_acceptance means:
    NOT ec_sacrifice AND a bounded immediate-mate acceptance witness.
It does not mean "an acceptance permits mate" for every position.

A legal replay audit of saved acceptance responses for all1,302 eligible cases
found immediate checkmate witnesses for61 misses,37 found Brilliants and22
ordinary moves. Of these, the mating_acceptance feature is false for:
    22 missed Brilliants,31 found Brilliants,11 ordinary moves.
Every one of these64 false-flag witnesses has EC true, explaining the masking.
Thus genuine saved information is not expressed independently to the forest.
The11 ordinary examples also show why adding an award exception would be wrong.
The scalar's masking is deliberate existing semantics, not a new extraction bug.

The helper also returns false on proof-budget exhaustion, rather than a separate
unknown value. That is a source-level limitation; this audit did not establish
that exhaustion caused any of the429 misses. Saved PVs are incomplete: lack of
a witness in the audit is not proof that no acceptance permits mate.

5. WHAT INFORMATION IS LOST, AND WHAT IS STILL UNPROVEN

The current representation has counts, flags, material totals, minimum/maximum
debts and gains, and a few root evaluations. It does not retain:

  - Which offered piece/capture enables which particular recovery or threat.
  - The target pieces/squares of a pin, fork, opened file or king attack, and
    the move order connecting the sacrifice to that compensation.
  - Side-specific material composition, beyond totals/phase and moved/offered
    role flags. Queenless exchanges and queen attacks can match those summaries.
  - Independent known/unknown outcomes for some conditional probes, including
    the masked mating witness above.

This information is visible in saved lines/boards; some could be represented
without new engine searches. But its usefulness for predicting the LABEL is not
yet established. The earlier response-gap and6/12-ply material-trajectory
experiments already failed the desired overall tradeoff. The ordinary examples
here have rich compensation too. A list of more tactical motifs is therefore
not justified as an award rule.

The best-supported direction for a future information experiment is a faithful
description linking an offer to its compensation and board context, rather than
assuming that more material debt, mate, or tactical uniqueness implies Brilliant.
The cleanest small encoding issue is to distinguish an actual mating witness
from EC masking and unknown proof status. Neither proposal has been tested here;
the masking issue alone covers only22 of the429 misses and cannot explain the
whole deficit. We have not separated limited tree capacity from feature limits
or insufficient training contrasts, and we have not measured a matching ceiling.

REPRODUCIBILITY AND LIMITS

Original forest reconstruction uses five deterministic outer fits/320 trees.
The first reconstruction attained exact parity but stopped on a count assertion:
it used the fixed-cutoff445 miss count where the requested selected-cutoff cohort
was429. The original script/protocol/stop record are preserved. One explicitly
authorized correction changed the diagnostic cohort mask, not the tree training
definition or thresholds, and reran the same five fits to serialize the trees.
Across both attempts there were10 reconstruction fits/640 trees; no new model
candidate or hyperparameter choice. The successful run took about9 seconds.

An independent read-only check recomputed all1,287 match choices across the three
control pools, all429 shared-leaf fractions, cohort/group restrictions, panel
selection, score summaries and recorded hashes. No discrepancies were found.
Path contributions sum back to the window score within5.56e-16.

This is repeatedly studied, enriched research data. Existing labels have mixed
historical provenance; the explicit-ordinary panel improves control clarity but
does not make it a fresh test. No rating inputs or analysis were used. No source
classifier, existing model, original data, prior study result, or deployment was
modified. No accuracy improvement is claimed.
