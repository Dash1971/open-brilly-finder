MATERIAL CHOICES IN THE 19 MATING-OFFER CASES
2026-11-04

Finding

Material accounting explains the chess more clearly, but does not separate
Chess.com's Brilliants from its Great/Best moves in these cases. In particular,
Brilliant does not consistently mean paying a larger material price than the
realistic alternatives. Some Brilliants preserve or win material against best
defense; one Great actually gives up a bishop to force mate. Nor does having a
strong alternative without an immediate offer consistently prevent Brilliant.

This is diagnosis of the same 19 known cases, not another evaluation trial.
All four correct additions, eight wrong additions, and seven missed Brilliants
remain included. No classification rule, forest, threshold, or application was
changed. Ratings were not used. These observations cannot establish Chess.com's
private algorithm or a general accuracy figure.

What we compared

For each original full-history position, Stockfish 18 searched the best four
moves at 3 million nodes. A separate search found the best move among all legal
roots without a qualifying immediate nonpawn offer, where that set was nonempty.
All actual moves appeared in the top four. There were 31 searches, completed in
84.55 seconds, with one thread, Hash64, cleared hash and fresh UCI game per query.
The script, input, engine and dependency hashes were frozen before execution.

The immediate-offer test is the existing bounded exchange calculation: an
opponent's legal capture of a nonpawn piece qualifies when its net same-square
exchange gain exceeds the mover's immediate capture credit by more than100.
There were no unknown root qualifications. Seven positions had no legal move
without such an offer. This does NOT prove an unavoidable material loss: a
capture may allow mate, and a check may merely postpone an offer.

We legally replayed 287 lines: both saved depth windows, all new candidate PVs,
the previously saved acceptance responses, and immediate acceptance/mate
witnesses. No new acceptance searches were added. Material counts start from
the ORIGINAL position, before the played move, so initial captures count as
credit. Values are pawn90, minor300, rook500, queen1000. These accounting units
are not engine centipawns. A transient deficit before a recapture is not a
lasting sacrifice. Nonterminal PV endpoints remain truncated evidence; mate
ends the game and is not evidence of a permanent material debt.

Contradictions that matter most

1. Genuine queen offer, but no paid queen sacrifice against best defense.
   Brilliant Nxe5 offers ...Bxd1 Bxf7#. Yet best defense is ...Qxe5:
   White captures a knight, Black captures that knight, and the original
   material balance is restored. Nbd2 also preserves material initially and
   scores +0.39 in the same search, versus +0.96 for Nxe5. Thus the Brilliant
   does not require a large evaluation improvement or material expenditure.

2. A Great really does pay for its mate.
   Great Qf7 meets ...Rxg2+ Rxg2 Bxg2 Qxh7#. White gives up a bishop and rook,
   takes a rook and pawn, and mates: a net bishop-for-pawn material concession.
   Qxc2, the next move found, is losing (-3.37). This is not an empty threat or
   an offer that the principal variation simply declines. Its material cost
   is more concrete than the Nxe5 Brilliant above, yet Chess.com says Great.

3. The two Qh5 moves remain closely matched despite opposite labels.
   Brilliant16.Qh5 leaves Bg5 offered: ...Bxg5 Qxf7#. Best13.Qh5 leaves Ne4
   offered: ...dxe4 Qf7# or Qg6#. Both offer a minor piece, both force mate,
   and neither best-defense mating PV drops below the original material count.
   Avoiding an immediate offer is worse in both positions: Bf4 +2.49 for the
   Brilliant, Nc3 -0.87 for the Best. The Best does have a stronger OTHER
   winning option, Rxf4 +5.38, but that move also leaves offers. This may help
   describe this pair; it does not explain h3, below.

4. New, undefended material exposure does not settle Rd8 versus Ba6.
   Brilliant Rd8 offers the moved rook: Qxd8 Qxg2#. Great Ba6 offers the moved
   bishop: Qxa6 Qd1#, while an already threatened rook also remains offered.
   Both actual moves are best and both have substantially worse alternatives.
   Against the saved strongest defenses, Rd8 wins a rook with ...Qxe2, whereas
   Ba6 eventually trades bishops and wins a pawn. Neither line establishes a
   lasting material expenditure by the player making the offer.
   The constrained Rd8 alternative begins Qf2+ and plays ...Rd8 two plies
   later: the filter can delay the same rook offer rather than avoid it.

5. Strong material-preserving alternatives do not consistently prevent Brilliant.
   Brilliant h3 forces mate in10, but cxd4 is already +8.16 in the constrained
   search. That alternative's saved line never falls below the original
   material balance and ends materially ahead; it eventually offers a rook
   nevertheless. This is a direct caution against a simple "already winning
   without the offer" interpretation. Ng4 also remains Brilliant despite
   Stockfish preferring Qh5. We have measured the disagreement, not explained
   Chess.com's internal evaluation or proved its label wrong.

6. Being forced to leave something offered occurs on both sides.
   No legal root avoids an immediate offer for Bxg3 and Nb8 (Brilliant), or
   Bh3, Qe6, Rxf2, Qf7 and the knight-fork Qf6 (Great). Bxg3 rescues a rook
   attacked by a knight by capturing that knight and exposing its own queen;
   best defense exchanges bishop for knight. Nb8's alternative Nb6 offers the
   same knight on another square. A binary "could avoid offering material"
   description therefore loses the essential transaction.

Case-by-case material ledger, including all contradictions

CORRECT ADDITIONS

Bxg3 (166826414194): The move collects a knight, offers the stationary queen,
and threatens mate if it is taken. Against Qxg3+ the bishop is traded for that
knight, restoring the original material balance. Ng7 instead permits Nxf5,
giving rook for knight. The Brilliant preserves material better in these lines.

Qh5 (173510361166): The already attacked bishop on g5 remains available.
Best defense Kg8 Bh7+ Kh8 Nxf7# declines it. Bf4 avoids an immediate offer,
but later trades minors. No material debt is required for the actual mate.

Qxg4 (169151816360): Captures a pawn and offers the moved queen. If ...Bxg4,
White is down910 accounting units relative to before the move, then mates.
Best defense ...Re8 declines the queen; the saved line keeps the extra pawn.
Ne2 and Qb3 are quieter, materially viable but weaker alternatives.

Rd8 (174182921870): Offers the moved rook, but strongest defense Re2 lets
Black capture that rook with Qxe2. The actual PV wins material. Qf2+ and a4
avoid an immediate offer and are far less effective; Qf2+ can postpone Rd8.

WRONG ADDITIONS

Bh3 (173487499542, Great): A knight already forks two rooks. The move leaves
both offered while creating mate. Best defense g3 Red8 leads to exchanges:
Bxf6 Qxf6 Nxa8 Rxd1 Rfxd1. Black's two rooks are exchanged for White's queen,
restoring the initial material balance with these piece values. Inferior
alternatives also leave an offer; this is a rescue of an existing threat.

Qe6 (167315486838, Great): The rook is already threatened. The queen move
threatens mate after Rxg7 but best defense Qxe6 fxe6 exchanges queens and
preserves the initial material balance. The other searched moves lose badly.
The queen's temporary disappearance before fxe6 is not a queen sacrifice.

Qf6 (179135871509, Best): Leaves an attacked bishop on g5. Best defense Nc3
declines it; Qxf5+ wins a minor. Nd2+ is a viable alternative without an
immediate offer, though its later exchanges differ between the two searches.
An immediately quiet root is not a guarantee of a quiet subsequent transaction.

Rxf2 (184007310619, Great): In check, White captures a knight and offers the
rook. Taking it permits Qxg7#. Best defense Rxe5 Rxe2 Qxe2 instead exchanges
knights and rooks, restoring the initial material balance. Every other legal
move loses to mate in this search. The apparent rook price ignores the knight
already collected and the opponent's alternative exchanges.

Qh5 (147424650238, Best): Leaves a knight offered, but the forced-mate PV
Qe8 Qxe8 Rg8 Qg6+ Kh8 Nf7# instead collects the enemy queen. Rxf4 and Bxf4
are already winning but still leave immediate offers. Retreating the offered
knight to Nc3 avoids an immediate offer but loses the advantage.

Qf7 (171360742446, Great): Actual best-defense mate pays a bishop for a pawn,
as described above. All roots leave an immediate offer. This remains a strong
counterexample to requiring an actual material concession for Brilliant.

Qf6 (171609570218, Great): A knight already forks queen and rook. Qf6 saves
the queen and guards the rook, leaving an exchange concession if Nxg7.
Best defense Nxe4 Nxe4 Rxe4 Rg5 Qh4 Qxf5 removes the forking knight and
recovers material. Qf7 offers a queen exchange and roughly equality; it too
leaves the rook available initially. The chosen move is materially stronger.

Ba6 (173700284016, Great): Newly offers a bishop while leaving a threatened
rook. Against Bd3 Rd8 a3 Bxd3 cxd3 Be7 Be3 Qxd3, the bishop is exchanged
and a pawn is won. The cleanest immediate retreat Rb8 is losing and still
allows a bishop to be taken later. This is not explained away as a fake offer.

MISSED BRILLIANTS

Nxf4 (169433595406): First wins a bishop; offers a rook. If Rxd8, the immediate
net loss is only200 accounting units, before any reply, because the bishop
is already collected. The saved best-defense PV trades queen for two rooks
and retains a material gain. Earlier acceptance analysis also found a winning
nonmating material route. That evidence remains; it was not excluded here.

Nxe5 (145479440308): Trades knights against best defense while offering a
separate queen, as described above. Nbd2 is a viable non-offering alternative.
Its later truncated PV ending down material is not a proof of forced loss.

h3 (172807397646): Leaves a bishop offered. Best defense Nf4 allows a pawn
advance, promotion, exchanges and mate without a deficit relative to the
original material balance. cxd4 also wins comfortably and preserves material
through its saved line, despite a later rook offer. Keep this contradiction.

Qxe3 (148103018608): Captures a bishop before offering the queen. Its immediate
accepted queen price is700 units relative to the original position, not1000;
fxe3 allows Bg3#. Best defense Nd5 Qh6 hxg4 Qxh1 instead trades bishops and
wins a rook. Bxf3 is nearly as strong but its continuation includes a later
unrecaptured bishop offer. Neither the immediate-offer filter nor one scalar
material difference fully describes these alternatives.

Ng4 (168956462236): Offers a knight, but best defense first allows Qxf1,
collecting a bishop; only then does hxg4 fxg4 occur. The saved actual PV never
falls below its original material balance. Qh5 is stronger, qualifies as no
immediate offer, and itself starts Qxc6+ bxc6: knight lost, queen gained.
The timing and total transaction matter more than the first captured piece.

d3 (145360060432): Leaves a knight offered, but strongest defense c3 Qd5 Bg2
Bxg2 fxe5 Bh3 Qf3 Qxe5 first lets Black take a bishop, then lose the knight,
then recover a pawn. It is a minor exchange followed by gains. Alternatives
Ng6/Ng4 are weaker and initially give up a pawn instead.

Nb8 (165547173668): Offers the moved knight; best defense Rc1 declines it.
Later Rxf8+ Nxf8 exchanges rooks, followed by Qxe5 losing a pawn. The stronger
Nb6 also offers the knight and its saved PV preserves material. All legal
moves leave an offer, so no clean non-offering comparison exists in this set.

What this investigation supports

We should describe the offered piece, the full exchange transaction, and the
quality/material consequences of alternatives separately. The current idea of
"material sacrifice" conflates them. This better description is useful for
understanding errors, but these cases do NOT yet support a new decision rule.
The strongest contradictory pairs remain unresolved, especially the two Qh5
moves, Rd8 versus Ba6, and Brilliant Nxe5 versus Great Qf7. Any future account
of Chess.com's distinction must explain those rather than discard them.
