Mute Chess: conditional response gap feature experiment
November 4, 2026 (Japan)

Outcome

Adding this one feature did not establish a useful replacement. It hurt the
custom forest and produced a small, uncertain gain in the standard forest.
Neither learner recognized more of the difficult ec_sacrifice=false positives
that motivated the hypothesis. Final training-only selection retained the
original expanded custom forest with the previously selected retuned cutoff.
No application model, setting, or deployment was changed.

Exactly what changed

For every case with at least one eligible engine window, enumerate all qualifying
accepting captures using the same legal-capture/SEE qualification as the pilot.
Search the position after each accepting capture, retaining full game history.
For each current/previous complete engine iteration, choose the opponent's best
acceptance: lowest resulting evaluation for the player making the original move.
The feature is the difference between that player's best and second-best reply
scores after that acceptance, in mover-relative centipawns.

Only this scalar and its missing-value indicator were appended to the original
150columns. The existing75raw measurements and75missing flags remained exactly
unchanged, in their original column positions. No new eligibility gate was added.
The feature was extracted irrespective of truth, cohort, model score or existing
En Croissant sacrifice feature. Missing values were not filled with zero.

When the chosen acceptance involves a mate score, fewer than two responses, or
an unavailable complete window, the gap is missing. Mate was not converted into
centipawns. A terminal outcome on a NONselected acceptance is a complete outcome
for branch ranking; it need not have two replies to allow a gap on the selected
nonterminal branch. The two engine iterations are correlated, not independent.

Evaluation controls

Same3724deduplicated moves,917positives,972game/position-group components.
Same five outer folds, three inner folds, salted group assignments, seeds,
window-row weighting, and award guards as the previous standard-learner study.

Custom:64trees, depth6, minimum8distinct moves per leaf,12candidate features,
whole-group bootstrap, cost5weighted splits and raw leaf fractions.
Standard RF:128trees, depth6,16unique window rows per leaf,12candidate features,
row bootstrap, no class weighting, one thread; scikit-learn1.7.2.
No hyperparameter sweeps, alternative feature definitions or outcome-driven
reruns were performed. Adding columns can change randomized tree splits, so
small differences are not proof of a stable causal improvement from the feature.

Inner thresholds maximize correct awards subject to no more wrong awards AND
no worse wrong-award fraction than the original fixed custom reference. Outer
labels never choose the method or threshold. Training constraints need not hold
exactly on unseen folds; failures are included in the reported totals.

Original custom and RF scores and original-arm award decisions reproduced the
previous run exactly. Source/protocol/feature/model hashes, grouping, absence of
ineligible awards, coverage and confusion counts passed verification.

Recommendation

Retain the saved feature/search evidence, but do not adopt this feature or expand
its search/model variants on the strength of this result. The target subgroup
remains unresolved. The next higher-value investigation is whether closely
matched Brilliant/ordinary cases differ in target award conditions, analysis
strength/evaluation, or player context, before adding more sacrifice descriptors.
This is a recommendation only; no label re-review or new study has been started.

Limitations

The corpus is enriched and repeatedly studied; sample precision is not a
deployment guarantee. The pilot supplied label-informed hypotheses. Even the
non-pilot breakdown is not independent validation. Only one representation of
conditional response gap and two fixed learner configurations were tested.
This result does not prove that counterfactual analysis can never help.
