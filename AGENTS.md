# Public research writing

- Use plain American English for a casual reader with no conversation context.
- Present the selected Mute Selective model as the result of one unified study. Lead with an abstract stating the measured achievement, then explain the method, comparison with other projects, and future research.
- Keep the reader’s path to one model and one clear conclusion. Do not lead with model versions, PR numbers, confirmation fixes, deployment coordination, sample-growth chronology, or iteration-by-iteration tables and charts.
- Explain the trained decision-tree forest and local browser execution plainly. Put exact thresholds, hashes, protocols and reproduction commands in technical reference pages.
- Keep denominators and evaluation limits accurate. Never pool different engine/sample/model results into an unsupported comparison or imply universal accuracy.
- Preserve source data, failed experiments and historical results in linked research appendices. The current README, report and charts must agree and describe the current selected model.
- Updating a report should revise the unified account, rather than stacking another dated addendum onto the reader narrative.
